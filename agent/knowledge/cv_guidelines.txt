Start the CV with a short summary that names the role the candidate is applying for and the strongest two or three qualifications.

List work experience in reverse chronological order, with the most recent role first. Each role needs a title, an employer, and dates.

Describe each role with short bullet points that start with an action verb, such as developed, managed, built, or led.

Put numbers in the bullets whenever the original CV gives them, such as team size, volume, or results. Never invent a number that is not in the original.

Put the skills that the job description asks for near the top of the CV, and make sure the wording matches the job description's terms when the candidate actually has the skill.

Education goes after experience for candidates with several years of work. Put education first only for recent graduates.

Remove details that do not help the application, such as hobbies unrelated to the job, long personal descriptions, and old roles that are not relevant.

Keep the CV to one or two pages. Use simple headings, a consistent format, and no decorative graphics.

Languages and certifications that are relevant to the job belong in their own short section.
